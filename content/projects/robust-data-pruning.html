Data selection · Adversarial robustness

Robust Data Pruning

How do the choices we make before, during, and after data selection affect a model’s robustness?

Imperial College London

Research with Dr. Seyed-Mohsen Moosavi-Dezfooli

Studying the full pruning process

We studied how data pruning affects adversarial robustness and whether selecting a subset of the training data can help produce a more robust model. We separated the pruning process into three stages and examined how the choices at each stage affect the final model.

  1. 01

    Prepare the data and compute statistics

    First, prepare the data and compute the statistics that will guide pruning. We examined how this preparation affects the information used to judge each example and, ultimately, the robustness of the trained model.

  2. 02

    Score examples and select a subset

    Next, use those statistics to assign scores and decide which examples to keep. We studied how the scoring rule and selection choices influence robustness when the model is trained on the retained data.

  3. 03

    Train on the pruned data

    Finally, train the model using the selected subset. We examined how the training procedure affects the robustness achieved with that subset, alongside the choices made in the first two stages.

By examining these stages separately, we aimed to understand where robustness is gained or lost and how data preparation, selection, and training work together.
