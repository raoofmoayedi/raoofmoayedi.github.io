Robust Prompt Tuning for Vision–Language Models

Can small changes to prompts improve robustness while preserving clean accuracy?

Nanyang Technological University

Research with Prof. Yew-Soon Ong

Robustness with limited adaptation

We investigated prompt tuning as a way to improve the adversarial robustness of vision–language models such as CLIP while retaining their accuracy on clean, unperturbed inputs. The appeal is to adapt a model through a small set of learned prompts, limiting how much of the pretrained model needs to change.

Methods such as TeCoA [1] and FARE [2] show that adversarial adaptation can make CLIP more robust. Their reported evaluations also illustrate a trade-off with clean accuracy: FARE preserves more clean performance than TeCoA in those comparisons, but can still fall below the original CLIP. This motivated us to investigate how prompt tuning could improve that balance.

Our research questions

Clean accuracy
Can prompt tuning retain or improve performance on unperturbed inputs?
Adversarial robustness
How can the prompts be learned so that the adapted model is more resistant to adversarial perturbations?
Limited changes
How much robustness can we gain through prompt-based adaptation while preserving the pretrained model’s capabilities?
